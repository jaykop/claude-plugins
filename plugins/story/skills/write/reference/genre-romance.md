# Genre: Romance

For stories where a relationship between two characters is the main line, and for romance mixed into another genre. Read it from step 1. Sources are in [sources.md](sources.md) under "Genres".

## The genre's promise

- What it promises the player: the relationship is confirmed, and it was built step by step through talk and choices.
- Success: the player can explain from their own choices why the two reached this ending. The ground is concrete scenes (remembering the other's taste, an alternative given up), not an affection number.
- Tone (pick one for the concept sheet): romance (an optimistic ending), melodrama (outside obstacles, pain and reward, a tragic ending is possible), romantic comedy (clash and misunderstanding, then reconciliation).

Genre formula in six cells (a design aid, not a test of what counts as romance):

| Cell | Romance |
|---|---|
| Characters | Two people and someone or something that gets in the way |
| Setting | Everyday spaces (work, school, the neighborhood), or a period or a crisis for subgenres |
| Conventions | A chance meeting, misunderstanding, reunion |
| Conflict | The main obstacle (below) |
| Resolution | Union, or a chosen parting |
| Value proved | That this love is real |

Reader expectations by subgenre:

| Subgenre | Expectation |
|---|---|
| Contemporary | Everyday spaces, a cool emotional distance. Avoid grand vows of forever |
| Romantic comedy | Two opposites clash, misunderstand, reconcile. The laughs come from bickering |
| Melodrama | Outside obstacles (social rules, war, rank), heightened emotion; the reward is love or recognition |
| Reunion | Parting → a gap → a medium → reunion. The first meeting's fantasy meets reality |
| Historical, paranormal, romantic suspense | A period, the supernatural, or rescue from danger as the backdrop. The relationship structure stays the same |

## Main obstacle

Pick one main obstacle and at most one secondary.

| Type | What keeps them apart |
|---|---|
| (a) Outside rules or evil | Power, corruption, family or social norms. Achieving the love is achieving the good |
| (b) Clash of character, class, values | They are opposites and find each other strange. Misunderstandings pile up and come undone |
| (c) Inner instability | Weak passion; neither is sure the other is the one. Friendship turning into love causes confusion |
| (d) An ideal from the past | A lost first love's fantasy makes the present person a shadow |

With several routes, give each route a different main obstacle. Two routes with the same obstacle feel the same.

## Relationship progress

Five stages:

1. Daily life and a trigger: the protagonist's lack or calling pulls them into the other's world.
2. First meeting: a reason appears for each to notice the other; their fit shows.
3. Approach: talk, learning tastes, a change of attitude.
4. Crisis: misunderstanding, an outside obstacle, an alternative appears, the ideal from the past.
5. Confirmation: one gives up the alternative and chooses the other. A happy ending, or a tragedy where neither gave up.

The relationship moves through talk, not touch. Four signals of progress; every scene that raises affection has at least one:

1. First clash: they meet in a disagreement, over something light.
2. Remembered taste: one remembers and mentions a small taste or habit of the other.
3. Changed attitude: one changes because of what the other said (the careless one becomes careful).
4. Confession with flaws: the confession lists the other's flaws too; they like the person knowing the flaws.

Design the pair as complements: decide one character, then design the other so each fills the other's lack. Show in lines or scenes why it has to be this person.

## Chance, misunderstanding, reunion

- Chance: allowed to bring them together, never to solve a crisis. Repeated chance feels like fate only with a gap between meetings. In a romance mixed with mystery, genre-mystery.md cheat 5 is stricter for the case itself: chance only as bad luck for the protagonist or good luck for the antagonist.
- Misunderstanding card: who knows / who does not / when it comes undone. The emotional peak is not the moment it comes undone but the moment one keeps going while knowing. Use the point where it comes undone as a choice (tell / hide).
- Reunion: first chance → a gap → a medium (a book, news, a place) → reunion. At parting, their means of finding each other are cut, and one medium brings them back.

## Ending

- Just before the final choice, one of them actually holds an alternative (the ideal type, a job elsewhere, escape) and lets it go. Without an alternative, it is just progress, not a confirmed love.
- Proof that the love holds differs by story: proving the other is good (moral awakening, sacrifice), personal care (knowing who the other is and fitting to them), or going away toward an ideal and coming back knowing whom one loved. Pick one and plant it early.
- A long route can be cut into chapters of "one day" each. Words from an earlier, happier day can return later as material for a fight.

## Mixing

- Romance as the main genre or as seasoning: decide in the concept sheet. As seasoning, one relationship beat per scene, and no grand vows.
- With mystery: the main genre decides the ending promise (the truth comes first). The romance uses romantic suspense conventions (rescue, digging up a secret).
- One ending promise only. Two ending promises need the user's agreement.
- Keep 3 or more conventions and vary 1. Give the form the player knows first, then break it in one place.

## In a VN

- Build affection from reason tags (remembered taste, stood by them in a crisis, caught lying), not one number. The format's variables record each tag.
- Make choices about talk ("mention what they like" / "pretend not to know"). Touch or confession alone does not raise affection.
- Split endings at the confirmation scene by flags (did an alternative exist, how often was a taste remembered). Without an alternative, the confirmation choice means nothing.

## Checklist (step 5 pass A)

1. Was the one-line promise kept at the end?
2. Does every affection-raising scene have one of the four progress signals?
3. Is there one main obstacle, and does it actually split them once?
4. Is the point where a misunderstanding comes undone a branch, and is there a scene where the relationship goes on after it?
5. Is there a scene just before the ending where someone holds an alternative and lets it go?
6. Is chance used only to bring them together, never to solve?
7. Do grand vows of forever stand in for the feeling?
8. With a mixed genre, is there only one ending promise?
