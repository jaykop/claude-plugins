# Presenting a story to a person

How to explain a story, a plot, a twist, or a scene list to the human: at every checkpoint, in chat, and in the summary at the top of a story document. The human judges the story from this text alone. If they cannot follow it, they cannot judge it.

## Rules

1. Write full sentences in the human's language. Terse or abbreviated output styles do not apply to a story pitch; clarity comes first.
2. Use this order:
   1. World: 2–3 sentences on who lives off whom and how (from the world order).
   2. What breaks and why now: 1–2 sentences, with the person who breaks it and what they want.
   3. Cast: one line each, with name, who they are, and what they want. Only the people the chain needs.
   4. The story in the order things happen: one paragraph per part or week. Every sentence says who does what, and why when the reason is not obvious.
   5. Twist, if any, as three lines: 플레이어가 믿는 것 / 실제로 일어난 것 / 알게 되는 장면.
   6. If the player sees events in another order, one paragraph: 플레이어는 이 순서로 본다.
   7. What the cold read still questions, and at most 3 questions for the human.
3. Introduce every name before using it, e.g. "강 씨(63, 야간 경비원)". Define every world term at first use (an organization's nickname, a code, a place name).
4. Tell events, not effects or craft. Do not write "농담처럼 지나간다", "복선을 깐다", "단서가 묻힌다", "분위기를 굳힌다", "장치", "비트", "회수", "뜻을 뒤집는다" in a pitch. Write what happens on screen and what each person does.
5. Do not refer to a scene by its effect ("경비실 떠보기 장면이 일상처럼 지나간다") before the human knows who is there, what is asked, and why it matters.
6. Tables belong in the document, not the pitch. The pitch is prose; point to the file for tables.
7. Length: up to about 25 sentences at checkpoint 1. Details live in the files.
8. If the human says they do not understand, do not resend the same text shorter. Find what was undefined or skipped, and retell from the world order in plain sentences.

A bad and a good pitch for the same story are in [../examples/story-chain.md](../examples/story-chain.md).

## Before sending

- Could someone who never saw the source or the conversation retell this? The cold read ([cold-read.md](cold-read.md)) answers that; do not skip it.
- Does any sentence name a person, group, or term that was not introduced above it?
- Does any sentence describe how a scene feels or what it plants instead of what happens?
